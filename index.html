Termites in Rockhampton: what the climate means

Rockhampton sits on the Tropic of Capricorn, with hot, humid summers and most of its rain from December to March. Bureau of Meteorology records for Rockhampton Aero average about 803 mm of rain a year. Heat and wet-season moisture suit termites, and CSIRO and Queensland Government advice puts Central Queensland in a high-risk part of the country.

CSIRO says termite risk on the Australian mainland generally increases further north, with the highest risk in the subtropics and tropics, and most states have a moderate to very high risk of attack. The QBCC recommends a termite inspection at least every 12 months, and more often in high-risk areas. CSIRO also recommends inspections at least once a year.

Signs to look for: mud tubes on stumps, slab edges or walls, timber that sounds hollow when tapped, mud packed into joints, and damp or stained areas. If you find termites, don't spray or break into the workings yourself. Leave them for the technician to identify.

Pest control licensing in Queensland

Last updated: 29 October 2026

In Queensland, pest management technicians need a pest management licence from Queensland Health under the Medicines and Poisons Act 2019. The licence lists what the holder is authorised to do, such as urban pests or timber pests. Chemical termite work also needs a Termite Management—Chemical licence from the QBCC.

Do I need a pest inspection when buying a home in Rockhampton?

It's strongly advised. A timber pest inspection to AS 4349.3 looks for termites, borers and timber decay, and the things that make an attack more likely. In Queensland, termite inspection reports and advice are covered by QBCC termite management licences, so ask whoever does a pre-purchase timber pest inspection for their QBCC licence number.
